# United States legal sources

Last reviewed: 20 September 2026.

Never treat “US law” as a single body of law. Identify whether the question is federal, State, territorial, tribal or mixed, and identify the relevant State and court system before drawing a conclusion.

## Federal legislation and regulation — official

- **[United States Code](https://uscode.house.gov/)** — codified federal statutes from the Office of the Law Revision Counsel.
- **[Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/)** — bills, public laws, legislative actions, committee material and legislative history.
- **[GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/)** — authenticated or official federal publications, including the Statutes at Large, Code of Federal Regulations and Federal Register collections.
- **[eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/)** — continuously updated editorial presentation of federal regulations. Check its status and any recent amendments against official Federal Register or GovInfo material where the distinction matters.
- **[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/)** — rules, proposed rules, notices and presidential documents; use the publication and effective-date information rather than a search snippet.

## Federal courts — official

- **[Supreme Court of the United States opinions](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/opinions.aspx)** — slip opinions, revised opinions and United States Reports material.
- **[United States Courts](https://www.uscourts.gov/)** — federal court structure, rules and links to the relevant Court of Appeals, District Court, Bankruptcy Court or specialised federal court.
- **Relevant federal court's official website** — local rules, standing orders, opinions and procedural material. Coverage differs by court.

## State law — official-first route

For every State-law issue:

1. identify the State and the relevant date;
2. use the official State legislature or code website;
3. use the official State judiciary and relevant court website;
4. use the official State administrative code, register and regulator; and
5. check local court rules where procedure matters.

Do not use federal sources as a substitute for State law, and do not assume that a rule or cause of action is uniform across States.

## Reputable free discovery sources

- **[CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/)** — case-law and citation discovery, opinion text and RECAP documents where available.
- **[Cornell Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/)** — readable federal materials, Supreme Court resources and links useful for discovery.
- **[Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/)** — federal and State case and code discovery.

These services are not substitutes for an official opinion, statute, rule or docket record where one is reasonably available.

## United States-specific checks

- Identify the deciding court, circuit and State; analyse federal and State hierarchy separately.
- Check whether the opinion is published, precedential, vacated, superseded, amended, reheard or reversed.
- Distinguish a syllabus, headnote or docket summary from the court's opinion.
- For statutes, distinguish codified text from the enacted public law where amendment, effective date or uncodified provisions matter.
- For regulations, check publication, effective date, stays, amendments and relevant agency or judicial action.
- Treat citator status as unavailable unless a connected source actually supplied it; do not infer “good law” from the absence of an obvious negative result.

## Preferred sequence

Federal: `official code or public law / official regulation / official court → CourtListener → Cornell LII or Justia → secondary commentary`

State: `official State legislature / judiciary / regulator → CourtListener → Cornell LII or Justia → secondary commentary`
