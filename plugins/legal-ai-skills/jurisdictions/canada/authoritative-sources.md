# Canada authoritative sources

Confirm the province or territory and federal nexus; distinguish Quebec civil law from common-law private law elsewhere, English/French authoritative texts and Indigenous legal interests. Never infer provincial law from a federal source.

## Primary-source starting points

- [Federal laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/)
- [Justice system and jurisdiction](https://www.justice.gc.ca/eng/csj-sjc/just/)
- [Supreme Court](https://www.scc-csc.ca/)
- [Federal Court](https://www.fct-cf.gc.ca/)
- [Quebec legislation](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/)
- [Ontario legislation](https://www.ontario.ca/laws)
- [British Columbia legislation](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/)
- [Privacy Commissioner](https://www.priv.gc.ca/)
- [Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

## Research and access rules

Official starting points were identified on 24 November 2026. The national legislation landing page was retrieved; individual provisions still require verification. Recheck access for each matter; these observations are not a promise of ongoing availability.

Retrieve actual legislation, gazettes, amendments, commencement instruments, judgments, rules and practice directions. Verify consolidated-through dates; an official portal can lag recent changes. For regional law use the relevant legislature, gazette and court; national text alone is insufficient. For specialist duties follow the responsible regulator's official instrument, distinguishing guidance from binding rules. Public judgments do not provide private docket or filing access.

Use the [central research protocol](../../integrations/legal-research.md). Record proposition, authority, pinpoint, material date/version, URL, retrieval date and status. Check adverse authority and later treatment where accessible. Secondary databases are discovery aids; disclose gaps in official text and citator coverage. Never invent a provision or infer verification from a search snippet.
