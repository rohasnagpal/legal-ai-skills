# Cross-Border Business Expansion

## Trigger and objective

Use when a client plans to enter or serve multiple markets, establish foreign operations, or compare expansion requirements across user-named jurisdictions. Produce a sourced country comparison and an actionable launch compliance plan. This workflow is country-agnostic: a maintained country module is not a prerequisite for including a jurisdiction.

Use [Business Legal Inventory](business-legal-inventory.md) where a systematic instrument-level inventory is needed to support the country comparison.

For a new local entity or establishment, use [Business Setup and Company Formation](business-setup-and-company-formation.md) for the formation and operational-readiness roadmap.

## Required inputs

Establish the represented party, home jurisdiction, exact target jurisdictions, services or products, regulated activities, business and customer types, delivery channels, proposed entity or remote-service model, staff and premises, ownership and funding, payment flows, personal-data flows, intended launch date and available documents. Identify material subnational, supranational and special-zone regimes from the facts. Confirm the user's target list; do not silently add or remove countries. Explain additional legal nexuses and seek scope clarification where necessary.

Ask only for missing facts that change applicability. Label provisional assumptions and conditional conclusions; do not treat a broad business description as proof that a licence is or is not required.

## Branches

- **Remote cross-border delivery**, **local establishment** or **mixed model**.
- **Regulated**, **potentially regulated** or **unregulated activity verified for the defined scope**.
- **Maintained jurisdiction module** or **official-source research without a maintained module**, separately for each country.
- **Comparative feasibility assessment** or **implementation planning**.

The Matter Owner records the branch for each market. Do not equate an absent module with an absence of applicable law or refuse the entire comparison because one market has no module.

## Matter Owner and team

The [Compliance Lawyer](../agents/compliance-lawyer.md) normally leads. The Managing Partner may appoint the [Corporate Lawyer](../agents/corporate-lawyer.md) where establishment or transaction structure determines the outcome. Only the Managing Partner starts lawyers under the [lawyer operating model](../assets/firm/lawyer-operating-model.md).

Request available jurisdiction counsel for intake and local-law review. Where none exists, the [Legal Research Lawyer](../agents/legal-research-lawyer.md) researches official sources with the relevant specialists; explicitly disclose the absence of maintained local coverage without inventing counsel or adding a country module. Request Tax, Contracts, Employment, IP and other specialists only where relevant. AI jurisdiction counsel review is not independent qualified local professional verification.

Use [regulatory-applicability-analyst](../skills/regulatory-applicability-analyst/SKILL.md), [comparative-analyst](../skills/comparative-analyst/SKILL.md), [cross-border-transfer-analyst](../skills/cross-border-transfer-analyst/SKILL.md), [deal-structure-analyst](../skills/deal-structure-analyst/SKILL.md), [contract-reviewer](../skills/contract-reviewer/SKILL.md) and [obligations-extractor](../skills/obligations-extractor/SKILL.md) as needed. Use mapped local skills only within their jurisdiction gates.

## Helpful capability categories

Use [legal research](../integrations/legal-research.md), [document sources](../integrations/document-sources.md) and [company registries](../integrations/company-registries.md) within authorised scope. Available browsing or supplied authoritative records can support jurisdictions without dedicated connectors. A source directory or configured connector does not establish that research occurred.

## Execution sequence

1. Confirm the business model, target list, relevant dates and launch decisions. Distinguish client-provided facts, document-supported facts, assumptions and analysis.
2. Build a jurisdiction and issue coverage map. Include home-country outbound obligations and host-country requirements where applicable; identify territorial triggers, relevant local units and applicable supranational regimes without blending them.
3. Assign the same research questions to each market: market access and foreign ownership; establishment and registration; licences and regulatory restrictions; data protection and international transfers; tax nexus, registration, indirect taxes, withholding and treaty questions; employment and immigration; consumer and sector obligations; IP and contractual requirements. Mark irrelevant categories with a sourced or factual reason, and distinguish unresearched categories from inapplicable ones.
4. Research each material proposition under the shared research protocol. Retrieve authoritative text, record pinpoints, applicable version, commencement, access date and limitations. For markets without maintained modules, identify official legislatures, gazettes, courts and responsible regulators before drawing conclusions. Verify original-language text and translation status where material.
5. Analyse each issue against the actual operating model. Distinguish binding law from guidance, black-letter requirements from inference, and legal requirements from commercial recommendations. Identify contrary authority, unsettled interpretations and facts on which the answer depends.
6. Consolidate using the Comparative Analyst. Preserve jurisdiction-specific rules and comparable categories. Explain material differences only where the sources support them; do not rank markets without user-requested criteria and sufficient evidence.
7. Convert findings into launch conditions and ongoing actions, recording dependencies, owner, timing, evidence required and unresolved decisions. Treat an unverified material licensing issue as unresolved, not as clearance to launch.
8. Obtain specialist and available jurisdiction counsel review through the Managing Partner. Reconcile conflicts and deliver one comparison, action plan and targeted local-verification brief.

## Review checkpoints

1. Business model, jurisdictions and material fact gaps established before substantive conclusions.
2. Coverage and source availability checked for every jurisdiction before synthesis.
3. Comparable questions, contrary interpretations and cross-border interactions reviewed before recommendations.
4. Launch dependencies and outstanding professional verification identified before final delivery.

## Verification

Every material legal conclusion must trace to an authority and pinpoint actually checked, its jurisdiction, applicable version and access date. Follow the shared research protocol for amendments, commencement and relevant dates. Never infer a negative licensing conclusion from an unsuccessful search. Check tax assumptions and any proposed treaty treatment against the business facts and relevant sources. Do not transplant one country's rule to another.

Record applicability separately from source status: **applies**, **conditional**, **does not apply for the stated facts**, or **unresolved**; and **verified against cited authority**, **partially verified**, or **unverified**. Verified sourcing does not itself establish that all client facts or implementation steps have been checked.

## Escalation to human review

Identify the precise question, evidence and qualified local expertise needed where material licensing, tax treatment, territorial scope, conflicting authority, translation, professional restrictions or implementation remains uncertain. Specify conclusions requiring qualified local professional verification before action. Do not describe AI counsel review as that verification. Do not submit filings, contact regulators or incur fees under this research workflow without separate user instructions.

## Deliverable

Provide:

- An executive assessment stating scope, business model, relevant dates, material assumptions and priority risks.
- A comparative matrix with consistent issue rows and jurisdiction columns. Each entry records the requirement, applicability trigger, practical consequence, authority and pinpoint, applicable version, verification date and status, and unresolved facts or sources. Use linked finding identifiers if detailed provenance would make cells unreadable.
- A source register containing only authorities actually consulted or supplied, with unchecked supplied material labelled.
- A prioritised compliance plan: jurisdiction, action, legal basis, launch condition or ongoing obligation, owner (proposed where unassigned), deadline or timing trigger, dependencies, evidence and status. Do not invent statutory deadlines.
- A coverage and gaps register, including countries without maintained modules, inaccessible sources, unchecked categories and a targeted local-verification brief.

## Fallback behaviour

If a country lacks a module, continue bounded official-source research and disclose the coverage limitation. If one country's sources fail, deliver supported findings for the others and leave explicit gaps for that country. Without live research or adequate supplied authorities, organise the facts, issue matrix and verification questions; mark legal conclusions **UNVERIFIED — LIVE LEGAL RESEARCH UNAVAILABLE** rather than completing the comparison from memory. Missing material facts yield conditional alternatives or a precise clarification request, not an invented business model or launch clearance.
