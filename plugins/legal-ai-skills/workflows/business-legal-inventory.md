# Business Legal Inventory

## Trigger and objective

Use when a user supplies a business description and asks which laws, rules, regulations or regulatory directions potentially apply, including across multiple operating countries. Discover relevant instruments systematically and explain each in one paragraph with an official source. Produce a scoped legal inventory and coverage review, not an unsupported assurance that every applicable law has been found.

For founder setup and formation planning, use [Business Setup and Company Formation](business-setup-and-company-formation.md) to turn this inventory into a setup roadmap.

## Required inputs

Establish the represented business, entities and group structure, operating and customer jurisdictions, relevant dates, products and services, sector, delivery channels, customer types, premises and assets, workforce and contractors, ownership and funding, payments, imports and exports, personal-data processing and transfers. Identify size, turnover, headcount and other thresholds only where they change applicability; do not invent values or silently assume exemptions.

Use the supplied description and documents first. Ask a consolidated set of material unanswered questions, including facts needed to identify national, subnational, municipal, supranational and special-zone rules. Do not choose countries for the user or silently narrow their list. Where additional legal nexuses emerge, explain them and clarify material scope uncertainty. Record missing facts and their effect on conclusions.

## Branches

- **Single-jurisdiction** or **multi-jurisdiction inventory**.
- **Existing operations**, **planned activities** or **both**, with dates kept separate.
- **Initial discovery**, **detailed obligation mapping** or **refresh of an existing inventory**.
- **Maintained jurisdiction module** or **official-source research without a maintained module**, per jurisdiction.

The Matter Owner records the scope and branches. An inventory of potentially applicable law does not establish compliance with it.

## Execution sequence

1. Convert the business description into a fact and activity map by entity, jurisdiction and relevant date. Separate user statements, document-supported facts, assumptions and analysis. Identify material gaps before drawing applicability conclusions.
2. Build a coverage checklist for every jurisdiction: corporate and registrations; sector licensing and regulator directions; tax and customs; employment, immigration and workplace safety; privacy, cybersecurity and transfers; consumer protection and advertising; competition; environment; premises, land use and municipal permissions; IP; commercial and electronic transactions; payments and financial regulation; trade, sanctions and import/export controls; and reporting or recordkeeping. Add categories the facts require. Record each as researched, pending, outside agreed scope or excluded with a reason. Absence of a familiar sector licence does not eliminate general business law.
3. Identify official legislatures, gazettes, courts and responsible regulators. Discover instruments from business activities, regulatory perimeter materials and cross-references in primary sources. Research subordinate rules, directions, orders, amendments and commencement provisions rather than stopping at principal Acts. Treat guidance separately from binding instruments; verify legal basis, addressee and scope before treating a direction as binding on the business.
4. Retrieve authoritative text under the shared research protocol. Verify territorial, entity, activity, customer and threshold triggers, exclusions, exemptions and relevant versions. Distinguish in-force duties from enacted but uncommenced provisions and proposals. Do not state a draft as current law. Verify original-language text and translation limitations where relevant.
5. Create one entry per instrument, linking its subordinate instruments separately and using stable identifiers to avoid duplicate entries. For each, write one paragraph explaining its subject, the specific business fact that triggers or may trigger application, principal practical obligations and material uncertainty. Cite supporting provisions and link directly to the official instrument or authoritative publication, not a search result or regulator homepage. Preserve an unavailable official link as a research gap, not a guessed URL.
6. Assign applicability and source verification independently. Applicability is **applies**, **potentially applies pending specified facts**, **excluded for the stated facts** or **unresolved**. Source status is **verified against cited authority**, **partially verified** or **unverified**. Preserve excluded candidates and reasons in a separate screening log so exclusions can be reviewed.
7. Map material obligations to the responsible entity, practical action, responsible function, timing or statutory trigger, evidence and priority. Identify dependencies and cross-border interactions without blending countries. Proposed owners must be labelled; do not invent dates or presume that a control exists or a breach occurred.
8. Review category coverage, source gaps, regulator boundaries, adverse interpretations and missing facts with relevant specialists and available jurisdiction counsel through the Managing Partner. Explain what was searched, what remains unchecked and how gaps affect reliance. A long list is not evidence of completeness.
9. Deliver the inventory and a targeted local-verification brief. For detailed control testing or remediation, hand findings to [Regulatory Compliance Review](regulatory-compliance-review.md); for expansion decisions use [Cross-Border Business Expansion](cross-border-business-expansion.md). For a refresh, compare against the supplied prior inventory, record additions, changes and removals with supporting sources, and preserve earlier versions. Do not create ongoing monitoring or alerts without a separate request.

## Review checkpoints

1. Business facts, jurisdiction list, dates and scope established before substantive applicability conclusions.
2. Category coverage and regulator/source map reviewed before declaring discovery complete within scope.
3. Applicability triggers, exclusions, subordinate instruments and verification status checked before obligation mapping.
4. Missing categories, sources, facts and required professional checks disclosed before delivery.

## Verification

Each material legal proposition must trace to a source actually consulted or supplied, its provision or pinpoint, jurisdiction, issuing body, applicable version, effective date where material and access date. Verify amendments, commencement and transition rules. Apply the shared research protocol for currency and contrary authority. An unsuccessful search is not proof of non-applicability or an exemption. Record why each exclusion follows from the facts and sources. Do not claim exhaustive coverage, legal compliance or professional local sign-off from AI review alone.

## Escalation to human review

Provide precise questions for qualified local review where applicability, thresholds, conflicting authority, inaccessible text, translations or regulator powers remain material and uncertain. Identify possible urgent licensing or reporting issues with sourced timing and the facts needing confirmation. AI jurisdiction counsel review is not independent qualified local professional verification. Contacting regulators, making filings, purchasing access or accepting compliance risk requires separate user instructions.

## Deliverable

Provide a scope statement and fact map, followed by:

- An instrument-level inventory grouped by jurisdiction and topic. Each entry has an identifier, official title and instrument type, one-paragraph explanation and business-specific applicability rationale, relevant provisions, direct official source, applicability status, version and verification date/status, and open questions.
- An obligation register linked to instrument identifiers, with responsible entity, action, responsible function, deadline or timing trigger, evidence, priority and dependencies. Distinguish legal requirements from recommended controls.
- A coverage checklist and screening log showing researched, pending, excluded and out-of-scope areas with reasons, plus inaccessible sources and material fact gaps.
- A source register containing only consulted or supplied material, with unchecked supplied material labelled, and a prioritised professional-verification plan.

State that completeness and accuracy remain subject to the disclosed scope, evidence and outstanding checks. Do not describe the inventory as a compliance certificate.

## Fallback behaviour

Missing business facts produce targeted questions and conditional candidates, not invented facts or definitive applicability. A country without a module remains eligible for official-source research. If one jurisdiction's sources are inaccessible, continue supported work elsewhere and leave explicit gaps. Without live research or adequate authoritative records, provide a provisional issue framework and mark legal conclusions **UNVERIFIED — LIVE LEGAL RESEARCH UNAVAILABLE**. Supplied authoritative records can support bounded findings, with currency limitations disclosed. Never fill the inventory from memory or fabricate official links.
