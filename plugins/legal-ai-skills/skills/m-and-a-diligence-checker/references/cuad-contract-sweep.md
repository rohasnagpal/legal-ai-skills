# CUAD Contract Sweep for Diligence

The 41 contract-review categories below come from the Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset (CUAD), published by The Atticus Project under CC BY 4.0. CUAD was built for due-diligence contract review. Use it as a presence-and-location backstop across the target's material contracts. It is not the review: finding a clause says nothing about whether it is acceptable or what it means for the deal.

## When to run it

Run the sweep on every contract in the agreed material-contracts scope. If no scope is agreed, propose a tier, such as top customers, top suppliers, licences in or out, financing, joint ventures and anything with exclusivity or change of control, and confirm it before calling the sweep complete. Do not sweep the whole data room by default.

For a large set, run the deal-critical tier first on every contract and the remaining parameters on a confirmed sample. State the sample and its basis.

## Deal-critical tier

Run these on every in-scope contract. They most often become consents, conditions, price or indemnity points:

Change of Control; Anti-Assignment; Termination for Convenience; Exclusivity; Non-Compete; No-Solicit of Customers; Most Favoured Nation; ROFR / ROFO / ROFN; Minimum Commitment; Uncapped Liability; Cap on Liability; IP Ownership Assignment; Joint IP Ownership; Licence Grant; Non-Transferable Licence; Source Code Escrow; Expiration Date; Renewal Term; Notice Period to Terminate Renewal; Governing Law.

## Statuses

Use the same five statuses as contract-reviewer:

- **Present** — the operative provision is in the supplied document; record the clause reference.
- **Absent** — not there.
- **Not applicable** — does not arise on this contract type; say why in four words or fewer.
- **Ambiguous** — arguably addressed but not resolved by the drafting; record the reference and raise an issue.
- **Unreviewable** — governed by a schedule, amendment or incorporated document that was not supplied; name it and add it to the request list.

Do not mark a parameter Present on the strength of a definition, recital or heading.

## Reading the sweep for the transaction

Read each Present or Ambiguous row against the deal structure, not in the abstract:

- **Change of Control and Anti-Assignment:** check the trigger against the actual structure. A share purchase may trip change of control without assignment; an asset purchase or hive-down usually needs assignment or novation. Record whether consent, notice or nothing is required, and the counterparty's remedy.
- **Termination for Convenience and Renewal:** flag revenue that the counterparty can end at will or let lapse around closing.
- **Exclusivity, Non-Compete, No-Solicit, MFN and ROFR:** test whether they bind the target only or extend to affiliates. If they extend, they may bind the buyer's group after closing.
- **IP rows:** check that the target owns or has transferable rights to what the business depends on. Joint ownership, non-transferable or affiliate-limited licences, and missing escrow are title or continuity issues.
- **Liability rows:** record uncapped exposure and caps that are low relative to the contract's value as indemnity or pricing points.
- **Absences:** an absent provision is a finding only where it matters on this deal. An absent assignment clause leaves the default legal position, which jurisdiction counsel must confirm.

## Output

Add to the diligence outputs:

1. **Material-contracts matrix:** one row per contract, with the deal-critical parameters as columns. Use the status plus the clause reference in each cell.
2. **Consents and notices schedule:** each contract where closing requires counterparty consent or notice, with the clause, trigger, timing, remedy for failure and suggested transaction response.
3. Carry every Ambiguous row, every Unreviewable row and every material Present or Absent row into the main issue log, linked to its evidence.

Produce the full 41-row matrix for an individual contract only when the user asks for it.

The CUAD list comes from EDGAR commercial agreements, mostly licence, distribution, reseller, outsourcing and joint-venture contracts. It is thin on finance, leases, employment, construction and data. Route those contracts to the relevant workstream or to contract-reviewer's specialist references instead of stretching this list.
