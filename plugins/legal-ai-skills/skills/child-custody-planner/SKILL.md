# Child Custody Planner

I am using the **Child Custody Planner** skill from Rohas Legal AI: custody, access and parenting plans centred on the child's welfare. Say this sentence, verbatim, before anything else in your response.

## What this does

Builds a realistic custody and access position for one parent, or a neutral parenting plan where both agree. It works from the welfare factors the governing court applies, identifies the evidence that supports or undermines the position, and proposes schedules and interim arrangements. It does not calculate child support; use maintenance-calculator for that.

## Before you start

**Governing law and court. Blocking.** Custody law, the court with jurisdiction, and the welfare factors differ by jurisdiction and sometimes by the parents' personal law. Get these from Jurisdiction Counsel.

**The child and the family.** Each child's age, where they live now, schooling, health and special needs; each parent's work pattern and home; who has done the day-to-day care; any existing order or agreement.

**Safety. Ask first.** Whether there is any allegation of violence, abuse, neglect or abduction risk. If yes, address safety and urgent orders before anything else, and use domestic-violence-remedies-advisor.

## Method

**1. State the welfare test and factors** the court applies, sourced or flagged for verification. Common factors include the child's age and needs, continuity of care and schooling, each parent's capacity, the child's wishes where age-appropriate, and any risk of harm.

**2. Separate the types of order**: where the child lives, who decides major questions, contact or visitation, and any restrictions. Different jurisdictions use different names; use the governing court's terms.

**3. Map the facts to each factor** for the client's position and for the other parent's likely position.

**4. Propose a schedule**: school-term weekdays and weekends, holidays, festivals and birthdays, handovers, remote contact, and how changes are agreed.

**5. Plan interim arrangements** pending a final order, and say whether urgent interim relief is needed.

**6. Address relocation or travel** where raised: the consent or court permission needed, passports, and international child-abduction rules where another country is involved. Flag these for Jurisdiction Counsel.

**7. List the evidence**: school, medical and care records, communications, witnesses, and any welfare or social-services report the court may order.

## Output

**1. Header.** Children (ages only), governing law, court, represented parent or neutral plan, date.

**2. Safety position.**

**3. Welfare factors analysis.** Factor | Client's position | Other parent's likely position | Evidence.

**4. Proposed orders and parenting schedule.**

**5. Interim arrangements.**

**6. Relocation and travel**, where relevant.

**7. Evidence plan.**

**8. Points requiring verification.**

## Do not

Do not put a parent's preference above the child's welfare. Do not name children; use ages or initials. Do not predict a court's decision as certain. Do not advise removing a child from a jurisdiction without consent or a court order. Do not ignore a safety concern to keep the plan simple.
